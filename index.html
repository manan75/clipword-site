How it works

  1. 1

    Select

    Select the sentence in your browser or email app and tap Save to Clipword. In apps without that option, copy it: Clipword offers what you copied the next time you open it.

  2. 2

    Tap the word

    A sheet slides up over the app you're in. Tap the word you didn't know. The whole sentence is kept with it.

  3. 3

    Review later

    A few minutes a day: see the word in its sentence, tap Forgot or Got it. Clipword works out when to show it next.

Small app, the parts that matter

Your sentence stays on the card

You remember words better in the place you met them. Every card shows its sentence with the word highlighted.

Dictionary first, AI second

Gender, plural, reading and dictionary form come from an offline dictionary. AI explains what the word means in your sentence, and you can edit anything.

Works offline

Saving, looking up and reviewing words all work without a connection. Explanations fill in when you're back online.

Two buttons, smart timing

No "hard/easy" guessing. A modern spaced-repetition scheduler (FSRS) picks the right day for each word, and daily limits keep the pile small.

Your words live on your phone

No account to make. Back up any time, or export to an Anki-ready file. Your cards are yours.

Hear it

Tap to hear a word read aloud, German nouns with their article, using your phone's own voices.

Questions

When can I try it?

A closed test on Google Play comes first. People on the waitlist get the invite. There's no fixed date yet; it's a small, careful build.

Is it free?

Saving your own words, reviewing them and exporting them will always be free. AI explanations and extras may become part of a paid plan later.

What about iPhone?

Android only for now. The "select text → save" step is built on an Android feature, so that's where Clipword starts.

Does it work in every app?

The Save to Clipword option shows up when you select text in browsers like Chrome and in many email and reading apps. Some apps (WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, most PDF viewers) only allow copying. There, copy the text and open Clipword; it offers what you copied as a card.

Do I need an account?

No. Your cards stay on your phone. Only the word and its sentence are sent when you ask for an AI explanation, and they aren't stored.

What happens to my email?

It's used only to invite you to the test and tell you when the app launches. Never shared, never sold. Details on the privacy page.
